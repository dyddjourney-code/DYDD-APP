Applied Capacity
Capacity is Grace given us in specific areas. What comes easily, joyfully, and fruitfully. Vs. What creates anxiety, strain, or fatigue? Capacity is also what you have energy for, and when you run out. Capacity helps you understand where grace flows naturally, where it stretches you, and where God may be inviting you into rest, boundaries, or partnership.

Drained (0–9): Grace that invites rest & partnership
Limited (10–24): Grace that cultivates dependence
Steady (25–39): Grace that sustains for endurance
Abundant (40–60): Grace that empowers sharp focus
What if you could use your DesignID scores to better manage your time?
There is a simple formula that allows you to gain a lens for interpreting how you use your time. It centers on the idea of capacity being what you have energy for and when you run out.
We assume the rule of 8’s. You sleep for 8 hours, work for 8 hours, and do the rest of life in 8 hours. I know that is only ideal and many of you have not seen that balance, ever. Feel free to play with the model to suit yourself.
We also acknowledge that many of you will discover that you spend a great more time doing things outside of your DesignID capacity. These numbers do not mean that you are not capable, they simply indicate the potential lines at which you lose interest, get fatigued, or are forced to push through. The numbers also help point at when you feel fulfilled in your work, and not.
Take a look at your numbers, which are simply calculated by: 16 hours X specific reflection score / 100.

So, what can you do with this?
Remember, these numbers represent 16 hours of your day. In other words, your work and home time. If you use up all your Shepherd hours at work, you may struggle when you get home. If you want to figure out how to just balance your 8 hours of work, just divide each number by 2 and think about how that plays out in your work day.
Enrichment activity: Your Calendar
Select one normal week of work and color code the weeks activities by DesignID reflection colors. Just do your best to guess at which reflection fits each item.
Here is a simple approach:
Architect: Completed tasks, submissions, presentations, sign off
Artisan: Strategy, design, improvement, assessment
Shepherd: Social, meetings, debate, mission,
Steward: Details, research, proof, organize
Add the total time spent in each activity for the week or a day and compare it to your own capacity. You might learn more about why you don’t like Thursday….
