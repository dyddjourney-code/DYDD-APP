# DYDD App Streamlining Plan - Base Camp to Ranger Station

## Product Read

Base Camp should become the welcome foyer, not the operating dashboard. When a new person signs in, they should see only enough to feel welcomed, recognize that their account exists, and know the one clear next action: go to Ranger Station.

Ranger Station should become the decision and orientation surface. It can carry the map, the guide prompts, and the first meaningful choice without making the learner decide from a full left-menu system before they understand the journey.

## Recommended First-Run Sequence

1. Website, social, referral, or word-of-mouth creates interest.
2. User reaches a clear public page explaining what DYDD is for and why the app exists.
3. User creates an account.
4. User lands at Base Camp.
5. Base Camp welcomes them by name, confirms account basics, and sends them to Ranger Station.
6. Ranger Station explains the map and asks what kind of start fits them.
7. User chooses a quick track or the full DYDD journey.
8. The chosen track unlocks the next page, tool, course, or report path.
9. Results, badges, courses, and journal artifacts start appearing back in the app.
10. Dydi/companion guidance becomes contextual based on where they are.

## Base Camp Direction

Keep Base Camp intentionally quiet:

- Welcome by first name.
- Show name and email only.
- Show a clean empty purchases/access state if nothing has been bought yet.
- Use one strong call to action: "Let's go to Ranger Station" or "Launch the tool."
- Avoid exposing every app destination before the learner understands the map.

Later, once auth/state supports it, consider hiding or soft-locking the broader side menu on a brand-new account until the person enters Ranger Station the first time.

## Ranger Station Direction

Ranger Station should answer one question: "Where should I start?"

Use the park map as the center, but pair it with a short welcome block and three or four quick tracks. The page should not explain every product in detail. It should guide the next click.

Recommended quick tracks:

- Free first step: Spiritual Gifts Assessment.
- Check my formation: FruitLife 360.
- Understand my design: DesignID.
- Walk the full path: Discover Your Divine Design Journey.

Secondary tracks can stay visible but less prominent:

- Not ready yet: listen at Fireside, browse Waypoints, and see if this is a good fit.
- Do this with others: invite a spouse, friend, group, or class through Camp Circles.
- Continue after DesignID: Design Pathways and DesignPD.

## Menu Philosophy

The app should feel like a guided trail, not a website menu. The left menu can still exist for returning users, but first-time users should be guided by state:

- New account: Base Camp and Ranger Station are primary.
- No purchases/results: show quick tracks and orientation.
- Assessment started: show current tool and status.
- Assessment complete: show report, next recommended step, and related course.
- Full DYD journey active: show the journey map, next lesson, and reflection checkpoint.
- Group/couple/class active: show Camp Circles and shared journey controls.

## Companion Guidance

Dydi should eventually act like the persistent contextual guide. The copy should change by stage:

- Base Camp: "Ready to choose where to begin?"
- Ranger Station: "Want help picking the right first trail?"
- Spiritual Gifts: "Want to understand how your gifts may serve others?"
- FruitLife 360: "Want to invite observers or interpret what is visible right now?"
- DesignID: "Want help reading your reflection blend?"
- DYD Journey: "Want help with this chapter's reflection?"
- Camp Circles: "Want to walk this with someone else?"

## Next Build Pass

The next practical pass should be small:

1. Tighten Ranger Station hero copy.
2. Add quick-track cards around the map.
3. Add a "not ready yet" low-pressure path to Fireside/Waypoints.
4. Add a "walk with someone" path to Camp Circles.
5. Decide whether brand-new users see a restricted menu until they enter Ranger Station.

Do not rebuild every page yet. Prove the first five minutes of the user journey first.
